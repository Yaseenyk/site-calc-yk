The compound interest formula

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

  • P is the principal amount invested.
  • r is the annual interest rate as a decimal (8% is 0.08).
  • n is how many times a year interest compounds (1 annually, 4 quarterly, 12 monthly).
  • t is the time in years.

Compounding means interest is added to the balance and then earns interest itself. The interest earned is A minus P.

How to calculate compound interest

  1. Divide the rate by the periods. 8% compounded annually: 0.08 / 1 = 0.08. Monthly: 0.08 / 12 = 0.006667.
  2. Count the periods. 10 years x 1 = 10 periods, or 10 x 12 = 120 for monthly.
  3. Raise (1 + r/n) to the number of periods and multiply by the principal.

Worked example: ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 10 years, compounded annually, grows to ₹2,15,892. That is ₹1,15,892 of interest. Compounded monthly, the same 8% grows it to ₹2,21,964.

Simple interest compared

Simple interest is P x r x t, with no interest on interest. On ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 10 years it is ₹1,00,000 x 0.08 x 10 = ₹80,000, so the total is ₹1,80,000. Compound interest gives ₹2,15,892, which is ₹35,892 more. The gap widens every year, because the compounding balance keeps growing.

How long to double your money

The rule of 72 is a shortcut: years to double are about 72 divided by the rate. The exact figure for annual compounding is ln 2 / ln(1 + r).

What this leaves out

It assumes one fixed rate and no deposits or withdrawals, and it does not include tax on the interest or any fees. Real products can use day counts or rounding rules that change the last few rupees. Frequently asked questions

What is the compound interest formula?

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is the principal, r is the annual rate as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the time in years. The total interest earned is A minus P.

How do I calculate compound interest on 1 lakh for 10 years?

At an example rate of 8% compounded annually, ₹1,00,000 grows to ₹2,15,892 in 10 years, which is ₹1,15,892 of interest. Compounded monthly it grows to ₹2,21,964.

What is the difference between compound and simple interest?

Simple interest is worked out on the original principal only, so it adds the same amount each year. Compound interest is worked out on the principal plus the interest already earned. On ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 10 years, simple interest gives ₹1,80,000 and annual compounding gives ₹2,15,892.

Does compounding frequency make a big difference?

It makes some difference, but less than the rate or the time. At 8% for 10 years on ₹1,00,000, annual compounding gives ₹2,15,892 and monthly gives ₹2,21,964, a gap of ₹6,072. Raising the rate by two points to 10% changes the annual result by far more.

What is the effective annual rate?

The yearly rate that gives the same result as the stated rate compounded more often. 8% compounded monthly has an effective annual rate of 8.3%. The calculator shows it as a tile.

How long does it take to double my money?

At 8% compounded annually, a little over 9.0 years. The rule of 72 estimates it as 72 divided by the rate: 72 / 8 = 9 years.

How do I find the time needed to reach a target?

Rearrange the formula: t = ln(A / P) / (n x ln(1 + r/n)). To grow ₹1,00,000 to ₹2,00,000 at 8% compounded annually, t = ln(2) / ln(1.08) = 9.01 years.

Which products pay compound interest?

A deposit or bond that adds its interest to the balance and then earns interest on it compounds, at the frequency its terms state. A mutual fund does not pay a fixed rate; for market-linked, assumed returns on monthly investments, use the SIP calculator.

Why does my bank's figure differ slightly from this calculator?

A bank can use the actual number of days in a period or round at each compounding date, which gives small differences from the textbook formula used here. Your deposit receipt or account terms have the exact method.
