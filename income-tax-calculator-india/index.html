Rates apply only to the slice of income inside each range, not the whole amount. The old regime allows Sections 80C and 80D (modelled here) plus others this calculator doesn't cover, such as HRA and home loan interest, and its standard deduction is ₹50,000 for salaried income. Its Section 87A rebate cancels tax only up to ₹5,00,000 taxable income.

What this estimate leaves out

This is a slab-and-rebate estimate, not a filing. It does not include surcharge on incomes above ₹50 lakh, the exact marginal relief just above either rebate threshold, special rates on capital gains or similar income, or old-regime deductions beyond Sections 80C and 80D such as HRA, home loan interest or NPS. Use it to plan, and a CA or the Income Tax Department's own calculator for a return you're filing.

Frequently asked questions

Is income up to ₹12 lakh really tax-free in the new regime?

Yes, for FY 2026-27. The slab rates on taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 add up to ₹60,000 of tax, and the Section 87A rebate cancels exactly that amount, so the net tax is zero. For a salaried taxpayer, the ₹75,000 standard deduction pushes the effective tax-free gross income up to ₹12,75,000.

Did Budget 2026 change the income tax slabs?

No. Union Budget 2026-27 (presented 1 February 2026) made no change to the income tax slabs, standard deduction or Section 87A rebate for either regime, so the FY 2025-26 figures carry over unchanged to FY 2026-27.

Which regime should I choose?

The new regime is the default and usually works out better if you claim few deductions. The old regime can work out better if your Section 80C, 80D and other deductions (including HRA and home loan interest, not modelled in this calculator) are large relative to your income. Run both and compare.

What is the Section 87A rebate?

A rebate that cancels tax up to a cap, provided taxable income is under a threshold: up to ₹60,000 for taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 in the new regime, or up to ₹12,500 for taxable income up to ₹5,00,000 in the old regime. Just above either threshold, marginal relief (not modelled here) limits how fast the tax reappears.

Is the standard deduction the same in both regimes?

No. It's ₹75,000 in the new regime and ₹50,000 in the old regime, and either way it only applies to salaried income, not business or other income.
