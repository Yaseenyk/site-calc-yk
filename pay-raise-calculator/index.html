How to calculate a pay raise

Three modes cover the usual questions:

  • Raise by %: new pay = current pay x (1 + raise / 100). It also shows what your pay becomes if you get the same percentage raise each year for five years, compounding.
  • Raise by amount: new pay = current pay + raise. The calculator reports the raise as a percentage of your current pay.
  • Find the % raise: raise % = (new pay - current pay) / current pay x 100. A negative result means a pay cut.

Worked example: a 3% raise on $60,000. $60,000 x 1.03 = $61,800, an increase of $1,800 a year. Going the other way, from $60,000 to $62,500 is a 4.17% raise.

Is your raise a real raise?

Prices change too, so a raise that matches the rise in prices leaves your buying power unchanged. The real change is (1 + raise) / (1 + inflation) - 1. The inflation rates below are examples to show the arithmetic, not forecasts; use the figure that applies to you.

What this calculator leaves out

It works on gross pay. A raise adds less to take-home pay than to gross pay because tax and payroll deductions apply to it. To see the per-period effect, convert the new figure with the salary calculator, and to see the after-tax effect use the paycheck calculator with the old and new salary.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage raise?

Multiply your current pay by 1 plus the raise as a decimal. A 3% raise on $50,000 is $50,000 x 1.03 = $51,500, an increase of $1,500.

How do I find what percent raise I got?

Subtract your old pay from your new pay, divide by your old pay, and multiply by 100. Going from $50,000 to $55,000 is ($55,000 - $50,000) / $50,000 x 100 = 10%.

What percent is a $5,000 raise?

It depends on the starting pay. On $50,000 it is 10%; on $80,000 it is 6.25%; on $100,000 it is 5%.

Does a raise work the same for hourly pay?

Yes. The percentage is applied to the rate, so a 4% raise on $20.00 an hour gives $20.80 an hour. Multiply by your weekly hours to see the effect on a paycheck: at 40 hours that is $32.00 more a week.

How much more per paycheck is a raise?

Divide the yearly increase by the number of paychecks. A raise from $60,000 to $61,800 adds $69.23 to each bi-weekly check and $34.62 a week, before tax.

Is the result before or after tax?

Before. The calculator works on gross pay. Your take-home increase will be smaller because income tax and payroll deductions apply to the extra pay. The paycheck calculator shows the after-tax difference.

How big does a raise need to be to keep up with inflation?

At least as large as the rate prices are rising. If prices rise 3% and your pay rises 3%, your buying power is unchanged. A raise of 2% when prices rise 3% is a real change of -0.97%, which is a small pay cut in buying power.

If my pay was cut, what raise gets me back?

More than the size of the cut, because the raise applies to a smaller number. After a 10% cut you need a 11.11% raise to get back to where you were.

What is a compounding raise?

The same percentage raise each year, each applied to the previous year's pay. Three years of 3% on $60,000 gives $65,564, which is more than three separate $1,800 increases would give ($65,400).
