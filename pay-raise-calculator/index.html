How the calculator works

Three modes cover the usual questions:

  • Raise by %: new pay = current pay x (1 + raise / 100). It also shows what your pay becomes if you get the same percentage raise each year for five years, compounding.
  • Raise by $ amount: new pay = current pay + raise. The calculator reports the raise as a percentage of your current pay.
  • Find the % raise: raise % = (new pay - current pay) / current pay x 100. A negative result means a pay cut.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage raise?

Multiply your current pay by 1 plus the raise as a decimal. A 3% raise on $50,000 is $50,000 x 1.03 = $51,500, an increase of $1,500.

How do I find what percent raise I got?

Subtract your old pay from your new pay, divide by your old pay, and multiply by 100. Going from $50,000 to $55,000 is ($55,000 - $50,000) / $50,000 x 100 = 10%.

Does a raise work the same for hourly pay?

Yes. The percentage is applied to the rate, so a 4% raise on $20.00 an hour gives $20.80 an hour. Multiply by your weekly hours to see the effect on a paycheck.

Is the result before or after tax?

Before. The calculator works on gross pay. Your take-home increase will be smaller because income tax and payroll deductions apply to the extra pay.

How much should a raise be to keep up with inflation?

At least as large as the inflation rate. If prices rise 3% and your pay rises 3%, your buying power is unchanged; a smaller raise is a real pay cut.
