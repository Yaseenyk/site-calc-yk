  1. Enter the amount you want to convert.

  3. Read the converted amount and the rate. Use Swap currencies to reverse the direction.

How a currency conversion is calculated

Converted amount = amount x exchange rate. The rate says how much of the second currency one unit of the first buys. The reverse rate is one divided by it.

Rates are quoted against one reference currency. To convert from currency A to currency B, the amount is divided by A's rate and multiplied by B's rate, which gives the same answer as the direct A to B rate. Worked example, with made-up rates for the arithmetic only: if 1 US dollar is 0.8 euros and 90 rupees, then 5,000 euros is 5000 / 0.8 = 6,250 dollars, and that is 6,250 x 90 = ₹5,62,500. The direct euro to rupee rate is 90 / 0.8 = 112.5.

What a margin costs you

The rate shown here is the mid-market rate. A bank, card or transfer app usually gives you a rate below it, a margin, and may add a fixed fee on top. What this converter leaves out

It shows the mid-market reference rate, not a rate you can trade at. It does not include provider margins, fixed fees, or taxes on a transfer. The rate comes from a free feed updated about once a day, so it can be a few hours behind the market. Use it to plan and check, and confirm the final figure with your provider.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert one currency to another?

Multiply the amount by the exchange rate from the first currency to the second. If 1 euro buys 1.25 US dollars (an example rate), then 50 euros is 50 x 1.25 = 62.5 dollars. The converter does this with live rates.

How do I convert between two currencies that are not the dollar?

Rates are quoted against one reference currency, so the converter goes through it. With example rates of 1 USD = 0.8 EUR and 1 USD = 90 INR, 5,000 EUR is 5000 / 0.8 x 90 = ₹5,62,500. The direct EUR to INR rate is 90 / 0.8 = 112.5.

How do I find the reverse rate?

Divide 1 by the rate. If 1 US dollar is 90 rupees (an example), 1 rupee is 1 / 90 = 0.0111 dollars. The converter shows both directions under the result.

Which currencies can I convert?

Any two currencies in the lists, including US dollars, euros, pounds, rupees, Australian and Canadian dollars, dirhams, yen and many more. The pickers show the currencies your browser knows, with the popular ones first. A currency with no published rate shows a message instead of a result.

Where do the exchange rates come from?

From a free public rates feed that publishes mid-market reference rates about once a day, with two backup feeds. The date of the rates is shown under each result.

Is this the rate I will get at a bank or exchange counter?

No. This is the mid-market rate. Banks, card networks and transfer apps apply their own margin or fee, so the amount you receive is lower. Compare the final amount, not the headline rate.

How do I check whether a transfer is good value?

Divide the amount that arrives by the amount you sent to get the rate you really got, then compare it with the mid-market rate shown here. For example, if 1,000 dollars arrives as ₹88,650 when the example rate is 90, you got 88.65 rupees a dollar, which is 1.5% below the mid-market rate. Include any fixed fee in the amount.

Why does the rate change during the day?

Currencies trade continuously, so rates move. The feed this page uses publishes about once a day, so the rate shown can differ from a live market quote and from the rate a provider shows you.

What if the rates will not load?

If none of the feeds can be reached, the converter lets you type a rate yourself, so you can still do the sum.
