How a paycheck is calculated

  1. Start with gross pay, the amount before anything is taken out.
  2. Subtract pre-tax deductions. Retirement contributions such as a 401(k) lower the pay your income tax is based on. Health premiums and similar benefits also lower the pay Social Security and Medicare are based on.
  3. Subtract the standard deduction to get taxable income ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household in 2026).
  4. Apply the tax brackets. Each rate applies only to the slice of income inside its bracket, not to all of it.
  5. Add Social Security and Medicare (together called FICA).
  6. Add state and local tax, then any after-tax deductions. What is left is take-home pay.

Social Security and Medicare

  • Social Security: 6.2% of wages, up to $184,500 a year in 2026. Pay above that is not charged Social Security tax.
  • Medicare: 1.45% of all wages, plus an extra 0.9% on wages above $200,000.
  • Your employer pays a matching share on top. It does not come out of your check.

State income tax

State rules differ, so this is the part to check against your own state. 9 states do not tax wages: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming. 15 states charge one flat rate, and the calculator fills those in when you pick the state. The rest use graduated rates, so enter your own percentage. Many cities and counties add a local tax, which you can include in the same box.

What this estimate leaves out

Real withholding depends on the Form W-4 you gave your employer and on IRS Publication 15-T, so a paycheck can differ from this estimate by a few dollars. The calculator does not include the child tax credit or other credits, extra income, overtime or bonuses, state standard deductions or exemptions, or payroll taxes some states add (such as state disability insurance). Use it to plan, and your pay stub for the exact figure.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my paycheck after taxes?

Take your gross pay, subtract pre-tax deductions such as a 401(k), subtract the standard deduction to find taxable income, and apply the federal brackets. Then add Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%), state and local tax, and any after-tax deductions. What remains is take-home pay. This calculator does all of it from your salary.

How much is taken out of a paycheck?

For a single filer earning $60,000, about 16 percent goes to federal tax, Social Security and Medicare combined, before any state tax. The share rises with income because the brackets are marginal, and falls with pre-tax deductions such as a 401(k).

Why is my real paycheck different from the estimate?

Employers withhold using your Form W-4 and the IRS payroll method in Publication 15-T, not your final tax bill. A different W-4, extra income, credits, local taxes or benefits not entered here all change the real figure. Your pay stub is the exact number.

Does a 401(k) contribution lower my tax?

A traditional 401(k) contribution lowers the pay your income tax is based on, so your take-home pay falls by less than the amount you contribute. It does not lower Social Security or Medicare tax. A Roth 401(k) is taken after tax and does not lower income tax today.

Which states have no income tax?

Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming do not tax wages. Pick one of them in the calculator and the state tax is set to zero.

Is bonus or overtime pay taxed differently?

Not at a special rate. They are added to your income and taxed through the same brackets, but employers often withhold a flat percentage from a bonus, which can make the check look smaller than the final tax owed.
