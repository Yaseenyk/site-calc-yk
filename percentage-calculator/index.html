The three formulas

  • X% of Y: result = Y x X / 100.
  • X is what % of Y: result = (X / Y) x 100.
  • Find the original value: original = result / (1 + percent change / 100).

Frequently asked questions

How do I find X percent of Y?

Multiply Y by X, then divide by 100. 25% of 80 is 80 x 25 / 100 = 20.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. 20 is what percent of 80? (20 / 80) x 100 = 25%.

How do I find the original value before a percent change?

Divide the result by (1 + the percent change / 100), using a negative number for a decrease. If 110 is the result of a 10% increase, the original was 110 / 1.10 = 100.

Why is percentage change different from percentage points?

A rate moving from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage point increase, but a 50% relative increase (5 / 10 x 100). The two describe the same move differently, and mixing them up is a common source of confusion in statistics about rates.

How is a percentage discount calculated?

Multiply the original price by the discount percent to find the amount off, then subtract it from the price, or equivalently multiply by (1 - discount / 100). A 20% discount on ₹500 takes off ₹100, for a final price of ₹400.
