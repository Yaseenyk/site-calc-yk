How a home loan EMI is worked out

A home loan EMI uses the standard reducing-balance formula: EMI = [P x R x (1 + R)^N] / [(1 + R)^N - 1]. P is the loan amount, R is the monthly rate (annual rate / 12 / 100) and N is the number of months. Home loans are usually the longest loan people take, so N is large (a 20-year loan is 240 months) and the total interest is large too.

Worked example: ₹35,00,000 at 8.5% for 20 years (240 months). The EMI is ₹30,374 a month. Over the full term you pay ₹72,89,715, of which ₹37,89,715 is interest, about 1.08 times the amount borrowed.

Why the early years are mostly interest

Interest is charged on the balance you still owe. At the start the balance is the whole loan, so most of each EMI is interest. On the example loan, year 1 pays ₹2,94,828 of interest and only ₹69,658 of principal, so the balance falls from ₹35,00,000 to ₹34,30,342. In year 20 the split reverses: ₹3,48,245 of principal and ₹16,241 of interest.

If the rate changes during the loan

On a floating-rate loan the rate can move. On the example loan, a rise of one percentage point (to 9.5%) takes the EMI from ₹30,374 to ₹32,625 if the 20-year tenure stays. If you keep paying ₹30,374, the loan runs for about 309 months instead of 240. Enter the new rate and the months left in the calculator to see your own case.

Tax on home loan interest

The Income Tax Department's guidance for salaried individuals (assessment year 2026-27) lists the Section 24(b) deduction for interest paid on a housing loan. For a self-occupied property under the old tax regime, the limit is ₹2,00,000 a year for a loan taken on or after 1 April 1999 for construction or purchase. Its list for the new tax regime shows the deduction for let-out property only. Which regime is better depends on your whole tax position; the income tax calculator compares the two. Confirm current rules with the department or a tax adviser.

What this leaves out

This estimate does not include processing fees, property or life insurance a lender bundles in, or a floating rate that changes during the loan. It does not model prepayments. For whether your income supports a given loan size, see the loan eligibility calculator. For a general loan, use the EMI calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How is a home loan EMI calculated?

The same reducing-balance formula as any loan: EMI = [P x R x (1 + R)^N] / [(1 + R)^N - 1], where P is the loan amount, R is the monthly interest rate and N is the number of months. For ₹35,00,000 at 8.5% for 20 years, the EMI is ₹30,374.

What is the EMI for a 50 lakh home loan?

At 8.5% for 20 years, ₹50,00,000 gives an EMI of ₹43,391. At the same rate for 30 years it is ₹38,446. The rate is an example; use your lender's rate.

How much of my EMI goes to interest in the early years?

A large share. On the ₹35,00,000 example, 81% of the first year's payments is interest. The share falls as the balance falls, and by year 20 the loan is mostly principal.

Does the down payment affect the EMI?

Yes. A larger down payment lowers the principal you borrow, which lowers the EMI and the total interest, for the same rate and tenure. Enter the loan amount after your down payment, not the property price. On a ₹50,00,000 property, a 20% down payment leaves a ₹40,00,000 loan.

Should I choose a longer tenure for a lower EMI?

A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises total interest. On ₹35,00,000 at 8.5%, 15 years costs ₹27,03,859 in interest and 25 years costs ₹49,54,884.

What happens if the interest rate rises by 1 percentage point?

On the example loan, the EMI for the same 20 years goes from ₹30,374 to ₹32,625. If you keep paying ₹30,374 instead, the loan takes about 309 months (25.8 years) to clear.

Is home loan interest tax-deductible in India?

It depends on your tax regime and the property. The Income Tax Department lists a deduction for interest on a housing loan under Section 24(b): for a self-occupied property under the old regime, up to ₹2,00,000 a year. Under the new regime its list shows the Section 24(b) deduction for let-out property only. Check the department's page and your own situation before relying on it.

Does the EMI include insurance and processing fees?

No. The calculator shows interest and principal only. Processing fees, property or life insurance a lender bundles in, and other charges are separate.

Can I see the month-by-month schedule?

Yes. Open the full amortization schedule under the year-by-year table in the calculator.
