What overtime pay is

Overtime pay is the higher rate paid for hours beyond a weekly limit. In the US the usual limit is 40 hours in a workweek and the usual rate is one and a half times your regular hourly rate, called time and a half.

Weekly pay = regular hours x rate + overtime hours x rate x multiplier. Regular hours are the hours up to the threshold (40 by default). Overtime hours are everything beyond it. Time and a half uses a multiplier of 1.5 and double time uses 2.

How to calculate overtime pay

  1. Split the week. Hours up to 40 are regular hours. Hours past 40 are overtime hours.
  2. Pay regular hours at your rate. 40 hours at $20 is $800.
  3. Pay overtime hours at the higher rate. $20 x 1.5 = $30, so each overtime hour pays $30.
  4. Add them. The two parts make the week's gross pay.

Worked example: 45 hours at $20. Regular pay is 40 x $20 = $800.00. Overtime pay is 5 x $30 = $150.00. The week comes to $950.00. For the rate on its own, or a chart of common wages, use the time and a half calculator.

The rules that decide whether you get overtime

The 40-hour week. The Fair Labor Standards Act says a covered employee may not work more than 40 hours in a workweek unless the extra hours are paid at “not less than one and one-half times the regular rate”.

Each week stands alone. The Act uses a single workweek as its standard and does not allow hours to be averaged over two or more weeks. A 30-hour week followed by a 50-hour week has 10 overtime hours in the second week.

The workweek. It is a fixed, regularly recurring period of 168 hours (seven consecutive 24-hour periods). It may begin on any day and at any hour, and once set it stays fixed.

Exempt jobs. Some salaried jobs are exempt from overtime. For the federal executive, administrative and professional exemptions, the employee must be paid on a salary basis of at least $684 a week, which is $35,568 a year, and must also meet a duties test. A job title or a salary alone does not make someone exempt. The guide to overtime pay covers the exemptions in more detail.

Overtime rules differ by state

The federal rule counts hours over 40 in a week. A state can go further. California, for example, requires:

  • one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 8 and up to 12 in a workday, and for the first 8 hours on the seventh consecutive day of work in a workweek; and
  • double the regular rate for hours over 12 in a workday, and for hours over 8 on that seventh day.

At $20 an hour, a 10-hour day in California is 8 x $20 + 2 x $30 = $220.00 before any weekly overtime. Other states have their own rules and exceptions, so check your state labor department. To model a daily rule here, set the threshold to the daily limit and enter that day's hours.

When a bonus changes your overtime rate

Overtime is based on your regular rate, which is your total pay for the week divided by the hours worked, not always the base wage. A bonus promised in advance, such as an attendance or production bonus, counts towards it. A bonus left entirely to the employer's discretion, in both whether and how much, does not.

The example in the federal regulations: a worker paid $12 an hour works 46 hours and earns a $46 production bonus. Straight-time pay is 46 x $12 = $552. Adding the bonus gives $598, and dividing by 46 hours gives a regular rate of $13.00. The 6 overtime hours earn an extra half of that rate: 6 x $6.50 = $39.00. The week's total is $637. This calculator does not do that step for you; it uses the single rate you type in.

What this calculator leaves out

It works on gross pay and one rate. It does not take off tax, handle two pay rates in the same week, add shift differentials or bonuses to the regular rate, or apply state daily overtime on its own. Treat the result as an estimate and check your payslip. For take-home pay, use the hourly paycheck calculator, and to turn a wage into a yearly figure use the hourly to salary calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How is overtime pay calculated?

Pay the regular rate for hours up to the overtime threshold, and the regular rate times the multiplier for hours beyond it. At $20 an hour, 45 hours with time and a half is 40 x $20 + 5 x $30 = $950.00.

How do I calculate overtime for a 50-hour week?

At $15 an hour: 40 hours x $15 = $600, plus 10 hours x $22.50 = $225, which is $825.00. If your employer pays double time instead, the 10 hours are worth $300 and the week is $900.00.

When does overtime start?

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, a covered employee must be paid at least one and one-half times the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. Some states add their own rules, such as California, which also counts hours over 8 in a workday.

Does the 40-hour rule average across two weeks?

No. The federal rule takes a single workweek as its standard and does not allow averaging over two or more weeks. If you work 30 hours one week and 50 the next, you are owed overtime for the 10 hours in the second week, even though the two weeks average 40.

What counts as a workweek?

A fixed, regularly recurring period of 168 hours: seven consecutive 24-hour periods. It does not have to be a calendar week and can start on any day and hour. Once your employer sets the start, it stays fixed.

Are salaried employees entitled to overtime?

It depends on whether the job is exempt, which turns on duties and pay, not on being paid a salary. For the federal white-collar exemptions the regulation currently sets a minimum salary of $684 a week ($35,568 a year), and the duties test must also be met. A salaried employee who is non-exempt is owed overtime.

Is a bonus part of the rate overtime is based on?

Often, yes. The federal regular rate includes all pay for the work except specific exclusions. A bonus that is promised in advance, such as an attendance or production bonus, is part of it. A bonus left entirely to the employer's discretion, in both whether it is paid and how much, is not. The example on this page works through one.

Is overtime taxed at a higher rate?

No. It is ordinary wages. A larger check can lead to more tax being withheld that period, but there is no special overtime tax rate. The time and a half calculator page covers a separate deduction for the extra half of overtime.

How do I enter double time or a daily rule?

Choose 2x in the multiplier list for double time. To model a daily rule, set the threshold to the daily limit (for example 8) and enter the hours for that one day, then add the days together.
