How GST is added and removed

Adding GST (price without tax known): GST amount = base x rate / 100; total = base + GST amount.

Removing GST (price with tax known): base = total / (1 + rate / 100); GST amount = total - base. This is the direction people get wrong most often: the rate was added to the base, so you divide the total by (1 + rate / 100). Taking the rate off the total overstates the GST.

Worked examples

  1. Add 18% to ₹1,000. GST is ₹180. Total is ₹1,180. For a sale within one state, that is ₹90 CGST and ₹90 SGST.
  2. Remove 18% from ₹1,180. Base = ₹1,180 / 1.18 = ₹1,000. GST = ₹1,180 - ₹1,000 = ₹180.
  3. The common mistake. ₹1,180 x 18% = ₹212.40, which would leave a base of ₹967.60. That is not the original ₹1,000.

GST rates in India

The GST Council's 56th meeting (3 September 2025) recommended replacing the four-tier rate structure with two main rates: a standard rate of 18% and a merit rate of 5%, plus a special rate of 40% for a select few goods and services. The changes on services, and on goods other than a few tobacco products, took effect on 22 September 2025. The 12% and 28% slabs that applied before are no longer used for new supplies of most items, but they still appear on invoices dated before the change. The rate for a particular item is set in the rate notification, so look the item up there; this page does not list items.

Which rate applies when a rate changes

When a rate changes, the rate follows the time of supply. The GST Council's FAQs on the 2025 changes work through cases such as goods supplied before the change but invoiced after it, and advances received before it. They refer to Section 14 of the CGST Act. If you have such an invoice, read those FAQs or ask a tax professional; the calculator only does the arithmetic once you choose the rate.

CGST, SGST and IGST

For a supply within one state, the GST is shown as CGST (central) and SGST (state), each half of the rate: at 18% that is 9% and 9%. For a supply between states, the same total is charged once as IGST. The GST amount is the same either way, which is why this calculator shows one GST figure and the even split beside it.

What this calculator leaves out

It does the arithmetic on one price at one rate. It does not know which rate applies to your item, it does not include input tax credit, cess or reverse charge, and it does not rework invoices that mix rates. For a percentage of any amount, use the percentage calculator; for income tax, the India income tax calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add GST to a price?

GST amount = price x (rate / 100); total = price + GST amount. For ₹1,000 at 18%, GST is ₹180 and the total is ₹1,180.

How do I remove GST from a price that already includes it?

Base price = total / (1 + rate / 100); GST amount = total - base price. For a ₹1,180 total at 18%, the base price is ₹1,000 and the GST portion is ₹180.

What are the GST rates in India now?

After the 56th GST Council meeting, most goods and services moved to a two-rate structure: a standard rate of 18% and a merit rate of 5%, with a special rate of 40% for a few goods and services. The changes took effect on 22 September 2025, except for cigarettes, chewing tobacco products and similar goods, which kept their existing rates until a later date to be notified.

Are the 12% and 28% GST rates still used?

Not for new supplies of most goods and services: the Council's changes replaced them with 5% and 18% (or 40% for some goods and services). They still appear on invoices for supplies made before 22 September 2025, so this calculator lists them as older rates. Which rate applies to a specific item is in the rate notification.

Which GST rate applies if the invoice date and the supply date are on different sides of a rate change?

The rate follows the time of supply rules in Section 14 of the CGST Act. The GST Council's FAQs explain how it works when goods or services were supplied before a change and invoiced after, and for advances. Read the FAQ linked in the sources, or ask a tax professional about your case.

What is the difference between CGST, SGST and IGST?

For a sale within one state, GST is shown as CGST (central) and SGST (state), each half of the total rate. For a sale between states, the full rate is charged once as IGST. Either way, the total tax is the same, which is why the GST amount in this calculator does not change.

How do I calculate 18% GST on 1,000?

₹1,000 x 18% = ₹180 of GST, so the total is ₹1,180. Split evenly, that is ₹90 CGST and ₹90 SGST within one state.

Why can't I just take 18% off a GST-inclusive price?

Because the 18% was added to the base price, not to the total. Taking 18% off ₹1,180 gives ₹967.60, which is wrong. The correct base is ₹1,000.00.

Can I use a rate that is not in the list?

Yes. Choose Other rate and type any percentage, for example a rate shown on an older invoice or a special rate for a particular item.
