What time and a half means

Time and a half is your regular hourly rate multiplied by 1.5. The “time” is your normal rate and the “half” is an extra 50% on top. At $20 an hour that is $20 + $10 = $30 an hour.

Time and a half rate = regular hourly rate x 1.5

How to calculate time and a half

  1. Find your regular hourly rate. If you are paid a salary, divide the yearly figure by 2,080 (40 hours x 52 weeks).
  2. Multiply it by 1.5. That is the rate for each overtime hour.
  3. Multiply by the overtime hours. That is your overtime pay before tax.

A worked week: 45 hours at $20 an hour, with overtime after 40. The first 40 hours pay 40 x $20 = $800. The 5 overtime hours pay 5 x $30 = $150. The week comes to $950, which is $50 more than 45 hours at the regular rate. To run a whole week like this, use the overtime pay calculator.

When the law requires time and a half

Federal rule. The Fair Labor Standards Act says a covered employee may not work more than 40 hours in a workweek unless the extra hours are paid at “not less than one and one-half times the regular rate”. The count is per workweek, not per day, and the federal rule does not add a premium for nights, weekends or holidays.

States can go further. California, for example, requires time and a half for hours over 8 and up to 12 in a workday and for the first 8 hours on the seventh day in a row of a workweek, and double time for hours over 12 in a workday and over 8 on that seventh day. Check your own state's labor department for the rule that applies to you.

Employer policy. Many employers pay time and a half or double time for holidays or hard-to-fill shifts by choice or by contract. That is on top of what the law requires, so the terms are in your contract or handbook.

Tax on the extra half

Overtime pay is ordinary wages. For tax years 2025 through 2028 the IRS allows a deduction for the pay that exceeds your regular rate, the “half” in time and a half, when that overtime is required by the Fair Labor Standards Act. The most you can deduct is $12,500 a year ($25,000 for joint filers), and it phases out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 for joint filers). At $20 an hour, $10 of each $30 overtime hour is the half that can count. It is claimed on your tax return, so read the IRS page below for who qualifies.

What this calculator leaves out

It works on gross pay and one hourly rate. It does not take off tax, and it does not handle a regular rate that includes bonuses, shift differentials or two different pay rates in one week, which can raise the rate overtime is based on. For take-home pay after tax, use the hourly paycheck calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is time and a half?

Pay at 1.5 times your regular hourly rate. If you earn $20 an hour, time and a half is $20 x 1.5 = $30 an hour. The extra $10 is the 'half'.

How do I calculate time and a half?

Multiply your hourly rate by 1.5. To find pay for overtime hours, multiply that rate by the hours: 5 hours at $30 is $150.

What is time and a half for $15 an hour?

$22.50 an hour ($15 x 1.5). At $18 an hour it is $27.00, and at $25 an hour it is $37.50. The chart on this page lists more rates.

Is time and a half the same as overtime?

Usually, but not always. Overtime is the hours beyond a limit, and time and a half is the usual rate paid for them. Some overtime is paid at double time, and some employers pay time and a half for hours that are not overtime, such as holidays.

What is double time?

Pay at twice your regular rate. At $20 an hour, double time is $40 an hour. It is set by employer policy, union contract or state law, not by the federal overtime rule.

Do I get time and a half for working holidays or weekends?

Not under federal law. The Fair Labor Standards Act only requires it for hours over 40 in a workweek. A premium for a holiday or weekend comes from your employer's policy or contract, unless those hours also take you past 40 for the week.

Do salaried employees get time and a half?

Only if the job is non-exempt. A salary alone does not remove the right to overtime; the exemption depends on the duties of the job and the pay level. An exempt salaried employee is not owed overtime under the federal rule.

How do I calculate time and a half from a salary?

First convert the salary to an hourly rate (annual salary / 2,080 for a standard year), then multiply by 1.5. A $60,000 salary is $28.85 an hour, so time and a half is about $43.27.

Is time-and-a-half pay taxed differently?

It is ordinary wages, but for tax years 2025 through 2028 the IRS allows a deduction for the 'half' portion of overtime required by the Fair Labor Standards Act, up to $12,500 a year ($25,000 for joint filers). The deduction phases out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 for joint filers).
